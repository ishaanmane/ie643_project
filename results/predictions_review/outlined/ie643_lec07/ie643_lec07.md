### Directional derivative:

For a fn. $f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$, the directional derivative along some **non-zero** direction $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$ at a point $x$ is defined as

$$f'(x; u) = \lim_{\alpha \downarrow 0} \frac{f(x + \alpha u) - f(x)}{\alpha}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page006 -->

## Gradient

Gradient of a fn at a point $x$

$$\nabla f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2} \\ \vdots \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_d} \end{bmatrix}$$

$$f'(x;u) = \langle \nabla f(x), u \rangle$$

$$u = -\nabla f(x) = -\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$$

If $\nabla f(x)$ ≠ zero vector:
$$f'(x;u) = \langle \nabla f(x), -\nabla f(x) \rangle$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page007 -->

## Descent Direction

$$f'(x; -\nabla f(x)) < 0$$

**Descent direction:**

A non-zero direction $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is called a descent direction of $f$ at $x$ if:
$$f'(x; u) < 0$$

![A 3D surface with axes $\eta_1$, $\eta_2$, $\eta_3$ (height axis) — a bowl-shaped contour plot with level curves labeled $w_1$, $w_2$, etc., illustrating the gradient direction toward the minimum.](figures/ie643_lec07_page007_b5.png){width=34.5325%}

## Negative Gradient Direction

**Negative gradient direction** $-\nabla f(x)$

step-length / learning rate.

![A wavy curve with an arrow pointing left from a marked point, illustrating the negative gradient direction.](figures/ie643_lec07_page008_b3.png){width=55.0000%}

$$f'(x,u) = \lim_{\alpha \downarrow 0} \frac{f(x+\alpha u)-f(x)}{\alpha}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page009 -->

# Property:

If $u$ (a non-zero vector) is a descent direction of fn $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ at $x$, then:

there exists some $\epsilon \geq 0$ such that for every $\alpha \in [0,\epsilon]$, $\lim_{\alpha\downarrow0}\frac{f(x+\alpha u)-f(x)}{\alpha}<0$

$$f(x+\alpha u)<f(x) \quad \Rightarrow \quad f'(x;u)<0$$

# Gradient Descent

$$\min_{w} E = \sum_{i=1}^{N} e(y^i, \hat{y}^i) \qquad \nabla E = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \nabla e(y^i, \hat{y}^i) \rightarrow \text{sample-based gradient}$$

full gradient → very large → overfit

- $w^0$
- for $k=0,1,2,\dots$
  - find descent dirn using $-\nabla E(w^k)$ ($10^{-8}$)
  - find a suitable step length $\alpha_k$
  - $w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + \alpha_k(-\nabla E(w^k))$
  - zero-gradient check to stop this process

$\epsilon_k$: fixed to be small value close to $0$; $10^{-5}, 10^{-4}, \dots$

$\alpha_k$: step-length finding procedures

# Stochastic Gradient Descent

$$D = \{(x^i,y^i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$$

$\rightarrow w^0$

$\rightarrow$ for $k=0,1,2,\dots$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;$\hookrightarrow$ Compute a **stochastic gradient**
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;$\rightarrow$ randomly choose $j\in\{1,2,3\dots N\}$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;$\rightarrow$ Compute $\nabla e(y^j,\hat{y}^j)$

$$w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + \eta_k \nabla e(y^j,\hat{y}^j)$$

Stochastic approx. 51: convergence to the local opt/saddle pt.

might not lead to descent

# Mini-batch SGD

$$D = \{(x^i, y^i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$$

$\rightarrow w^0$

$\rightarrow$ for $k=0,1,2,\dots$

$\hookrightarrow$ Sample mini-batch of samples:
$$B_k \subseteq \{1,2,\dots,N\}$$

for every $j \in B_k$: compute $\nabla e(y^j, \hat{y}^j)$

$\hookrightarrow$ $w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + \eta_k \left(-\sum_{j \in B_k} \nabla e(y^j, \hat{y}^j)\right)$

convergence in asymptotic sense

# Adam

**SGD with momentum**

$$m_k = \alpha \cdot \text{grad}_k + (1-\alpha) m_{k-1}$$

generalize perf

$$v^k = \alpha (\text{mini-batch gradient}) + (1-\alpha) \cdot \text{past gradients}$$

**Adam:**
$$w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k - \eta_k$$

AMSGrad:
$$\left( \frac{\text{running avg of gradients}}{\sqrt{\text{running avg of sq. grad.}} + \epsilon} \right)$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page015 -->

$$m_k = \beta_1 \cdot \text{grad}_k + (1-\beta_1) m_{k-1}$$
$$v_k = \beta_2 \cdot (\text{grad}_k^2) + (1-\beta) v_{k-1}$$

$$w^{k+1} \leftarrow w^k + \eta_k \left( -\frac{m_k}{\sqrt{v_k}+\epsilon} \right)$$

**Sample-based gradient**

$$\nabla e(y^j,\hat{y}^j)$$

## Forward Chain Rule

![A feedforward network — $j^{th}$ neuron in layer $l-1$ connects via weight $w_{ij}^l$ to $i^{th}$ neuron in layer $l$.](figures/ie643_lec07_page017_b1.png){width=55.0000%}

$i^{th}$ neuron in layer $l$

$j^{th}$ neuron in layer $l-1$

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial w_{ij}^l} = \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_i^l} \cdot \boxed{\frac{\partial z_i^l}{\partial w_{ij}^l}}$$

**forward chain rule**

$$z_i^l = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{l-1}} w_{ik}^l a_k^{l-1}$$
$$a_i^l = \phi(z_i^l)$$
$$= \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_i^l} a_j^{l-1}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page018 -->

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial z_i^l} = \left(\frac{\partial a_i^l}{\partial z_i^l}\right) \cdot \phi'(z_i^l) = \frac{\partial e}{\partial a_i^l} \cdot \phi'(z_i^l)$$

![A neuron $a_i^l$ (layer $l$) feeding into $N_{l+1}$ neurons of layer $l+1$, labeled $z_m^{l+1}, a_m^{l+1}$ ($m=1,\dots,N_{l+1}$).](figures/ie643_lec07_page018_b4.png){width=38.6918%}

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial a_i^l} = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot \left(\frac{\partial z_m^{l+1}}{\partial a_i^l}\right) = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$

<!-- page: ie643_lec07_page019 -->

## Chain Rule

$$z_m^{l+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_l} w_{mk}^{l+1} a_k^l$$

$$\frac{\partial e}{\partial a_i^l} = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$

$$= \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial a_m^{l+1}} \cdot \frac{\partial a_m^{l+1}}{\partial z_m^{l+1}} \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$

$$= \sum_{m=1}^{N_{l+1}} \frac{\partial e}{\partial a_m^{l+1}} \phi'(z_m^{l+1}) \cdot w_{mi}^{l+1}$$
